# Module 21.1, Week 10 Reflection

**Reasoning behind this round:** By Week 10 the eight functions had sorted into distinct regimes, and the queries this round followed directly from what each function had shown. F5 received a boundary-relaxation query with all four dimensions in [0.985, 0.999], continuing improvements building since the original upper boundary was breached in Week 6. The result was 8561, a fourth straight new best. F6 received its first query with x4 above 0.854, territory untested in nine prior rounds due to a Sobol artefact in the initial data. It returned -0.211, also a new best. F1's perpendicular probe returned 0.022, about 1.5% of the Week 7 peak; the Week 9 on-ridge probe had returned 46% from a point only 0.025 away along the ridge axis. That asymmetry resolved the geometry: a narrow ridge, not an isotropic hotspot. F4 reversed sharply from its Week 9 best of 0.492 down to 0.105 from the same neighbourhood, leaving open whether that result was stable or a lucky draw. The left panel of the chart shows the full method history: each cell is the acquisition strategy, the number is the portal result, and the star marks a new campaign best.


**Transparency and reproducibility:** The decision-making process is transparent to the extent that every submitted coordinate has a named kernel, Sobol seed, candidate pool size, and search-bound derivation on record. A researcher attempting to reproduce this round would need all of those, plus the combined dataset, initial data plus all ten weekly submissions loaded together, not just the current week, and the leave-one-out Q-squared comparison that selects the surrogate kernel before candidate generation.

That last step is where Week 10 exposed a gap. The kernel comparison is meant to run first, every week, before any candidates are generated. In Week 10, it did not. The code defaulted to RBF for most functions. The right panel of the chart shows the consequence: for five of the eight functions, the kernel actually used in Week 10 was not the one that leave-one-out cross-validation would have selected. The green bars show what the correct kernel achieves; the red bars show what RBF achieves. The gaps range from +0.016 for F2 to +0.086 for F8 in surrogate quality. A surrogate with Q² of 0.313 (RBF on F8) is materially weaker than one with Q² of 0.399 (Matern32). The acquisition scores generated from the weaker model directed the search differently than the better-fitting model would have. The coordinates submitted cannot be revised, but the gap is documented alongside the method, and the step has been made mandatory for Week 11 onward. That is what transparency means in practice: not a clean record, but a complete one.

**Assumptions shaping the strategy:** The GP surrogate assumes stationarity: one length scale characterises correlation across the entire domain. For functions with spatially varying structure, this will be wrong in some regions. F4's non-stationarity diagnostic flag and its Week 10 reversal are the clearest signal of where this assumption costs something real. The beta parameter governing the explore-exploit balance is set from a phase-based schedule rather than tuned to each function's specific landscape. That is a deliberate simplification, and it is documented, but it remains an assumption without formal justification.

**Gaps and coverage biases:** F6 is the most concrete example in the campaign. The x4 coordinate sat between 0.597 and 0.963 across all nine prior submissions, not by design but because a structural artefact in the initial Sobol draw set a ceiling that the GP surrogate then reinforced week after week. The first query above 0.854 produced a new best. Eight rounds of effective search space contraction had a real cost. F1's queries were similarly clustered, with no deliberate cross-axis test until Week 10. In 8 dimensions, F8's ten queries cover roughly 10% of the domain, sparse enough that confident GP predictions over most of the space are extrapolations, not interpolations.

**One significant limitation:** The surrogate kernel must be correctly selected and applied for the acquisition strategy to function as designed. When that step is skipped, the model driving candidate generation is not the best-fitting one available, it is whatever the code defaults to. The resulting coordinates are valid observations, but the strategy that produced them was operating with a weaker model than the data supports. That gap is invisible from the portal result alone. It is only detectable if the process record is complete enough to compare what was used against what should have been used. The chart makes that comparison explicit.
